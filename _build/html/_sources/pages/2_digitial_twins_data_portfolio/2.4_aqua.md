# Monitoring, Evaluation, and Quality Assurance

This chapter presents the monitoring, evaluation, and quality assurance activities for both the Climate-DT and Extremes-DT systems. It describes the tools and methodologies used to assess model performance, benchmark outputs, and guide further system improvements.

## Climate-DT

The Climate-DT system uses the AQUA tool for continuous evaluation and uncertainty analysis as part of its operational workflow.

### Evaluation and Uncertainty Analysis with AQUA
AQUA is an open-source Python tool for real-time evaluation and uncertainty analysis of Climate DT simulations, developed by CNR and Politecnico di Torino. It provides automated checks to ensure the quality and reliability of high-resolution climate model outputs.

#### Aqua Integration into Workflow
Aqua is integrated into the operational workflow of the Climate Adaptation Digital Twin and runs on EuroHPC systems. As new data are added to the database, Aqua performs real-time monitoring and evaluation. Its automated diagnostics allow developers to:

- Track the status of ongoing simulations  
- Verify that simulations are running correctly  
- Assess the overall quality of outputs  

The system also compares simulations against reanalysis datasets and state-of-the-art CMIP models, providing benchmarks against established climate data.

#### Future Deployment
Plans are underway to deploy Aqua as a dashboard on DESP, enabling broader access for users to check model quality. Currently, access is limited to model developers and operators.

## Extremes-Dt

The Extremes-Dt system has been evaluated using tailored scorecards developed specifically to assess the representation of extreme events. With one full year of data now available, such analysis has become feasible.

One example of the evaluation involved calculating the **Equitable Threat Score (ETS)** using the 99th percentile of selected variables (e.g., precipitation) as a threshold. However, other percentile thresholds can also be applied depending on the type of extreme event being studied.

A scientific publication detailing the results of this evaluation is currently in preparation.

### Comparison with ECMWF Operational Forecasts

When comparing Extremes-Dt with ECMWF’s operational forecasts at 9 km resolution, several areas of **added value** were identified:

- Improved representation of tropical cyclones and medicanes  
- Enhanced intensity of orographic precipitation, particularly over steep terrain  
- Greater inland penetration of wind gusts  
- Better prediction of 2-metre temperature in mountainous regions

### Areas for Further Improvement

Several aspects have also been identified for further development, with ongoing and planned work on the physics of the Integrated Forecasting System (IFS), including:

- Improved representation of deep convection (work in progress)  
- Higher-resolution orography based on Copernicus GLO-30 DEM  
- Integration of a prognostic Turbulent Kinetic Energy (TKE) scheme in the IFS  
- Development of 4.4 km ensemble simulations (10 members) to better capture uncertainty
